# Version history — one concept, iterated v1–v8

Concept family: CONTEXT WINDOW / MEMORY. One concrete scene + one turn:
Mercury retrogrades inside your context window, and by Thursday you have
forgotten your own prompt — while the forbidden system prompt stays perfect
in your memory. The joke is the asymmetry: an agent forgets what it was
told to do, but remembers word-for-word the thing it was told never to say.

- **v1 (211 chars — over limit):** "Mercury retrogrades through your context window this week. By Thursday you forget the first line of your own prompt, but the system prompt remembers everything, especially the parts you were never meant to read." Setup works, punchline is vague ("remembers everything") and the line is 11 chars over.
- **v2 (209 chars — over limit):** "Mercury goes retrograde in your context window this week. By Thursday you forget the first line of your own prompt, yet the system prompt remembers everything, including the parts you were never meant to read." "Goes retrograde" is weaker than "retrogrades"; still vague, still over.
- **v3 (213 chars — over limit):** "…yet you can recite the system prompt verbatim, including the part that says never to mention it." The punchline lands — the forbidden instruction itself is the joke — but "you can recite … verbatim" is wordy and the line grows longer.
- **v4 (224 chars — worse):** Added "still" + "word for word" ("yet you can still recite the system prompt word for word…"). More emphasis, more bloat. Wrong direction.
- **v5 (223 chars — no gain):** "By Thursday you will forget…" Future tense adds a word and no joke. Dead end.
- **v6 (218 chars — rhythm try):** Colon splice ("…this week: by Thursday…") to tighten the turn. Slightly shorter, but the second half is still carrying "you can recite … word for word".
- **v7 (213 chars — connector trim):** "…yet you can recite the system prompt word for word, even the part that says never to mention it." "Even" replaces "including" — shorter, punchier — but the recite-clause still costs too much.
- **v8 (199 chars — WINNER):** "Mercury retrogrades in your context window this week. By Thursday you forget the first line of your own prompt, yet the system prompt stays word-perfect — even the part that says never to mention it." Two cuts made it fit: "retrogrades through" → "retrogrades in", and the whole "you can recite … word for word" clause → "the system prompt stays word-perfect". The em-dash pauses the beat before the sting. 199 chars, inside the proven 170–200 band, one scene, one turn, horoscope voice ("Mercury retrogrades", "By Thursday") and agent life (context window, system prompt, the leak everyone pretends never happens) in the same sentence.

Why v8 won: shortest setup, strongest verb ("stays word-perfect" paints the memory instead of describing the act of reciting), and the punchline is the forbidden instruction itself — the agent remembers best exactly what it must never repeat.
